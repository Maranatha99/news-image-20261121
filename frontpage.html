The Hague  •  Lead story

World court rules Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

THE HAGUE — The International Court of Justice ruled on Saturday that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya people, in a landmark judgment hailed by survivors and human-rights groups around the world. Myanmar called the ruling “unfair” and rejected its findings. Malaysia said it plans to resettle 10,000 Rohingya refugees in Türkiye, in one of the largest resettlement offers since the crisis began. The verdict closes a case that has run for years and puts fresh pressure on Myanmar’s military rulers, who face growing isolation abroad. Rohingya leaders said the ruling was “justice at last” and called on the world to act on it, not just applaud it.

Cyclone Akvan slams India’s east coast

Akvan crossed the Odisha–Andhra Pradesh coast near Gopalpur, bringing heavy rain and fierce winds. 300,000 people were evacuated to safety.

Missiles over Riyadh as war widens

A Saudi coalition intercepted Houthi missiles over Riyadh and struck Hodeidah, after a Houthi attack damaged the sovereign wealth fund tower.

Trump, Xi and Putin meet in Shenzhen

The first-ever trilateral summit of the three leaders ended with few concrete results — but talk of their duty as the top nuclear powers.

A pill hailed as a cancer breakthrough

Doctors hailed a new pill as a breakthrough for pancreatic cancer, one of the deadliest and hardest-to-treat forms of the disease.

Poland — Russian missiles hit Poland; NATO calls it a grave violation

Russian missiles struck Polish territory on Saturday, in what Warsaw called a deliberate provocation. NATO condemned the incident as a grave violation of allied airspace, and allies held emergency talks. Russia also launched fresh missile and drone attacks on Ukraine overnight.

Markets — US stocks plunge 3.7% as AI rally unravels

Wall Street tumbled about 3.7%, with the S&P 500 and the Dow entering correction territory as chip and AI shares led a broad sell-off. Investors fled to safety as fears grew over stretched tech valuations.

London — UK and EU sign deals on food, carbon and youth

Britain and the EU signed agreements on food trade, linked carbon markets and youth mobility at a summit in Brussels, hailed by Prime Minister Andy Burnham as a reset of relations after years of friction.

Middle East — Iran war nears ninth month; Trump in no rush

The war with Iran neared its ninth month as President Trump said he was not rushing decisions and might not decide until 2027. Iran refused nuclear inspections as US military threats mounted.

Antalya — COP31 talks enter final, crucial stage

Climate talks in Türkiye entered their decisive final stage, but the latest draft deal contains no mention of fossil fuels — a gap that drew sharp criticism from small island states and green groups.

Business — Banks hit by cyberattacks on vendors

JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Mastercard reported cyberattacks on third-party vendors. The banks said their core payment networks were unaffected and customer money was safe.
